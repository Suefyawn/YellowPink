## 1. Why this is the priority

The technical/on-page/schema/content audit came back clean. Strong pages still
sit at position 50–70 with ~0 traffic for one reason: the domain has almost no
link authority. The gap vs. PK competitors is stark.

| Domain | Backlinks | Ref. domains | Authority Score |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **yellowpink.pk** | **2** | **2** | **2** |
| goldenpearl.com.pk | 16,638 | 599 | 15 |
| theordinary.pk | 396 | 117 | 12 |
| trego.pk | 232 | 97 | 14 |

Because we have only 2 referring domains, **every** domain linking to a
competitor is a gap opportunity. The lists below are mined from competitors'
actual referring domains (sorted by Authority Score), then filtered to the ones
that are *replicable* — i.e. a link we can realistically earn.

## 2. What to ignore (noise in the competitor profiles)

Don't chase these — they inflate competitor counts but are not real, earnable links:

- **Search-engine cache artifacts:** `yahoo.com`, `bing.com`. Auto-generated,
  not editorial. They'll appear for us too once we're indexed; no action.
- **Auto-generated SEO-tool pages:** `siteprice.org`, `seositecheckup.com`,
  `storeleads.app`, `fastbase.com`, `websitescrawl.com`, `otterly.ai`,
  `accio.com`, `semfirms.com`. These appear automatically when someone runs a
  tool on the domain — zero outreach value.
- **Link farm / PBN — actively avoid:** the cluster on IP `195.20.19.178`
  (Moldova) — `drjack.world`, `bye.fyi`, `jake.eu` — plus job-board spam
  (`jobsinaccounting.com.au`, `mortgageandfinance.jobs`). Paid/PBN links;
  buying these risks a Google penalty. **Do not pursue.**

## 5. Linkable data asset — "Vitamin D Deficiency in Pakistan"

A single strong, citable asset is the highest-leverage link magnet because it's
*referenceable* — bloggers and journalists link to data, not to product pages.
This one doubles as on-site content that ties straight to wellness SKUs (NB Sons
range, multivitamin, calcium combos).

> **Working title:** "Vitamin D Deficiency in Pakistan: What the Data Actually
> Says (2026)"
>
> **Publish at:** `/blog/vitamin-d-deficiency-pakistan` with original,
> republishable charts (republish-with-attribution = inbound links).

> **Demand-alignment note (see `docs/SEO-SEARCH-CONSOLE-2026-06.md` Finding 2):**
> the site's real ranking traction is in **PCOS / fertility / men's & women's
> reproductive health**, not skincare. A PCOS-led asset — e.g. *"PCOS in
> Pakistan: how common is it and what the evidence says"* — will pull links from
> the same health editors *and* land on the site's strongest topic cluster
> (Simfolic, M-Sol, myo-inositol, prenatal/fertility SKUs). Lead with PCOS or
> fertility; keep Vitamin D as a second asset.

**Anchor statistics (all peer-reviewed; cite these in the piece).** According to
PubMed:

- Pakistan has the **highest vitamin D deficiency in South Asia at ~73%**
  (95% CI 63–83%), above India (67%), Bangladesh (67%), Nepal (57%) and Sri
  Lanka (48%); South Asia pooled prevalence 68%, and deficiency is **higher in
  women than men**. Siddiqee et al., *BMC Public Health* 2021 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12889-021-11888-1).
- Among obese euglycemic women in Lahore, **>40% were vitamin D deficient**, and
  deficiency tracked with higher insulin resistance. Saleem et al., *BioMed
  Research International* 2021 — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/2021/2259711).
- Vitamin D deficiency aggravates menopausal health risks, where supplementation
  has a potential role. Sharma & Kalra, *J Pak Med Assoc* 2024 —
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.47391/JPMA.24-27).

**Structure:** (1) headline stat + chart; (2) why Pakistan is so high (sun
avoidance, dress, diet, latitude); (3) who's most at risk (women, PCOS,
menopause, obesity — ties to Simfolic/folic-acid and women's-health SKUs);
(4) what helps (testing, diet, supplementation — soft link to wellness category,
not hard-sell); (5) full reference list with DOIs.

> Note: keep it genuinely informational and medically careful — link earns trust
> *because* it isn't a sales page. One contextual link to a wellness resource
> page is enough.
